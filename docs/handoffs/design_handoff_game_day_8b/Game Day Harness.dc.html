The data is a simulated game, not convenient numbers: 8 players, 6 on the field, minimum 3 of 4 periods — 24 period-slots against exactly 8 × 3 = 24 needed, so the sit plan has zero aggregate slack and every kid sits exactly one period. Subs happen at the breaks, plus three mid-period wrinkles: Jeffrey off injured at 6:00 of P1 (Zendrix on early), Reyansh's two-minute nosebleed in P1 (Oliver covering), and Connor back on for a tired Neel a minute ago. Every column of the ledger sums to 6 × elapsed.

The verdict is one of four ranked states: ✓ min met · +0.6p spare (can still sit that long) · on from now (spare under half a period) · short 0.4p (unreachable). With a working rotation nobody ever goes short — the late-arrival state shows the one realistic way it happens.

Reading spare: spare = time left − time still needed. It starts at exactly +1.0p for everyone (4 periods − 3 required) and that is also its ceiling — a +1.4p cannot occur in this format. Minutes on the field don't move it; only bench minutes drain it, which makes it a sitting allowance: +1.0p = hasn't sat at all, +0.7p = has already sat 0.3p of their one-period allowance, 0 = allowance spent. The goal is to end the game at or above zero. The arithmetic does go negative, but a negative number is never printed: under half a period it prints as on from now (a value that small invites spending slack that real sub timing will eat), and below zero it becomes the cone short pill carrying the deficit's magnitude — "−0.4p spare" would bury the headline that the minimum is now unreachable.
